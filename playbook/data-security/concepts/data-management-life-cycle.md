# Data Management Life Cycle

## Overview
The data management life cycle covers how data is created or collected, stored, used, shared, archived, and eventually destroyed. Each stage requires security measures based on the sensitivity of the data and the risks involved.

## Key Concepts
- Creation and collection: Identify what data is needed and classify its sensitivity.
- Storage: Protect saved data through encryption and access restrictions.
- Use: Allow authorized users to access data for approved purposes.
- Sharing: Protect data during transfers and verify who can receive it.
- Archiving: Secure data retained for future use or retention requirements.
- Destruction: Securely dispose of data when it is no longer needed.

## Why It Matters
Data can be exposed or mishandled at any stage of its life cycle. Understanding each stage helps identify risks and choose safeguards that protect information from collection through disposal.

## Common Uses
- Assessing how an organization handles sensitive information.
- Developing data retention and disposal policies.
- Reviewing how information is stored and shared.
- Identifying unnecessary data that increases security risk.

## Best Practices
- Collect only the data needed for business purposes.
- Classify data based on its sensitivity.
- Restrict access according to job responsibilities.
- Encrypt sensitive data at rest and in transit.
- Establish retention periods based on business and legal requirements.
- Use secure disposal methods appropriate to the storage medium.
